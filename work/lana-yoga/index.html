Lana Yoga — Website Design

01 // PROJECT OVERVIEW

A Website for a Real Yoga Business

This was a freelance project to design a website for Lana Yoga — a yoga business led by instructor Lana. The website was intended to communicate her teaching approach, present her class offerings, explain membership options, and guide visitors toward taking the next step in their practice.

The design needed to feel calm, approachable, and credible — consistent with the overall tone of a wellness and yoga business. The goal was to give visitors a clear sense of who Lana is, what she offers, and how to get started.

CLIENT CONTEXT

Lana Yoga is a yoga studio offering live and on-demand classes, memberships, and international retreats. The site needed to serve multiple audiences — people new to yoga, those exploring memberships, and visitors interested in retreats — all within a single coherent experience.

02 // DESIGN GOALS

What the Design Was Asked to Achieve

These were the design objectives guiding the work — focused on presentation and communication, not assumed outcomes:

  • Present yoga offerings and class information clearly. Visitors should quickly understand what types of classes are available, the style of practice, and how to access them.
  • Communicate the instructor's approach and personality. The website needed to introduce Lana as a teacher — her values, her style, and why someone would want to learn from her.
  • Organise information so visitors can understand their options. Between live classes, on-demand content, memberships, and retreats, the structure needed to guide rather than overwhelm.
  • Make membership and booking-related calls to action easy to identify. Sign-up prompts and next steps needed to be visible and accessible at the right moments in the page flow.
  • Maintain a consistent visual experience across the website. Typography, colour, spacing, and component style needed to feel unified from page to page.
03 // WEBSITE STRUCTURE

Major Content Areas

The website is organised across several distinct content areas, each serving a different visitor intent:

01 — Homepage Primary introduction, hero section, community messaging, instructor overview, class highlights, and testimonials.
02 — Offerings Yoga class listings — including Beginner, Hatha Yoga, Prenatal, and Restorative — with schedule and booking access.
03 — Pain Free Pass Membership area presenting the chronic pain management programme, live + on-demand content, pricing, and sign-up.
04 — International Retreats Dedicated page for retreat programmes — locations, format, and how to apply or enquire.
05 — Free Classes Wednesday and Friday free morning classes — schedule, class grid, and video content access.
06 — Contact & FAQ Frequently asked questions section and contact access, with footer navigation across all sections.
04 // VISUAL DIRECTION

Colour, Typography & Tone

The visual direction of the website reflects the brand's natural, calm, and grounded identity. The palette draws from earthy and muted tones — sage greens, warm beiges, and soft backgrounds — to communicate stillness and trust without being clinical or corporate.

Typography supports legibility and warmth. The combination of a structured sans-serif for headings and a readable body font keeps the experience approachable. The overall visual tone is intentionally unhurried.

06 // KEY DESIGN DECISIONS

Design Choices Worth Noting

These are observations and decisions visible in the design that can be discussed with confidence:

  • Instructor-forward introduction. The homepage leads with a strong sense of Lana's identity and community — "Pain Free Community" and the "Welcome Home" section put her personality and mission front and centre before any class listings appear.
  • Navigation by intent. The main navigation separates Pain Free Pass, Offering, International Retreats, and Contact — each representing a distinct visitor path rather than just a page type. This keeps the structure goal-oriented.
  • Membership page layout. The Pain Free Pass page uses a visible inclusion list ("What's Included"), pricing comparison, and instructor bio to support the decision. This is structured to reduce friction for someone considering a commitment.
  • Free class accessibility. The free Wednesday and Friday morning classes are prominently presented with a class schedule — this decision prioritises community trust over immediate conversion.
  • Social proof placement. Testimonials and transformation stories are placed on the homepage below the class offerings, not buried in a separate section — making them visible during initial browsing.
  • CTA consistency. Sign-up buttons ("Sign Up For Free Classes", "Join Now", "Book") appear at consistent points throughout the page flow — at natural decision moments rather than only at the top or bottom.
07 // FROM DESIGN TO LIVE WEBSITE

Design Handoff & Implementation Differences

The developed website contains some differences compared to the original design submission. This is common in freelance website projects where design and development are handled by separate parties, or where practical implementation constraints shape the final result.

Some observable differences in the live site include: image presentation choices, spacing between certain sections, and the handling of some mobile layouts. These are noted factually — they reflect the realities of a design-to-development workflow.

08 // WHAT I LEARNED

Honest Reflections on the Work

Working on a real freelance project — with an actual client, a live audience, and a real business — teaches things that practice projects cannot replicate.

01.

Designing for a real business requires context. Before making visual decisions, I needed to understand who Lana's audience is, what problem she solves for them, and how people discover the site. Client work forces that discipline.

02.

Structure matters as much as aesthetics. Deciding what information goes where — what appears on the homepage versus a dedicated offering page — has a direct impact on how visitors navigate. Those decisions are design decisions, not just content decisions.

03.

The gap between design and implementation is real. Some details I designed were implemented differently. Understanding why — and how to design in a way that travels well through development — is a skill I'm continuing to develop.

04.

Responsive behaviour needs explicit design attention. Mobile layouts don't automatically follow from desktop designs. Reviewing the live site on mobile revealed areas where more explicit mobile design specifications would have helped.
